# Problem statement — "Small Green Habits"

**Nudging Real, Measurable Sustainable Behaviour**
Contributed by **Ecovolt Technologies** · Theme: Sustainability
Primary contact: **Glenn — glenn@ecovolt.ai** (mentors on site all event)

Source: `EV_LIFEHACK.pdf`, extracted verbatim 29 Aug 2026.

## Framing question

> How might we nudge ordinary people toward sustainable habits, around energy, water, waste, or
> consumption, in a way that produces **real, measurable behaviour change** rather than just good
> intentions?

## Background (their diagnosis)

Almost everyone agrees sustainability matters, and almost nobody changes their daily behaviour
because of it. The **guilt-and-lecture approach has largely failed** — dashboards, statistics and
stern reminders get ignored. The problem is **not a lack of information, it is a lack of
motivation and delight.**

Products that genuinely change behaviour at scale — streak counters, leaderboards, step
challenges, defaults that quietly do the right thing — work because they are designed around how
people actually decide and act.

> "Sustainability does not have a data problem; it has a behaviour problem. The tools that measure
> resource use are everywhere now, but data alone never changes a habit; a person does. This
> challenge asks you to design **the human layer**."

## The challenge

Build something that nudges ordinary people toward a more sustainable everyday habit, in any
resource domain: **energy, water, waste, food, transport, or consumption**.

Required of the solution:
- Pick **one specific, measurable behaviour** to shift (e.g. shorter showers, correct recycling,
  unplugging idle devices, skipping single-use plastic, walking over riding)
- Pick **a clear audience** (students, office workers, families, a dorm, a neighbourhood)
- Apply **a deliberate behaviour-change mechanic**: a nudge, an incentive, social proof, a
  commitment device, a well-timed reminder, or a default that quietly does the right thing
- **Define how success would be measured**: what behaviour, tracked how, improved by how much

> **"Engagement is a means, not the goal: a mechanic is only as good as the change it produces."**

### Hard constraints
1. Must be **usable end to end by a real person**
2. Must target **one concrete** sustainable behaviour
3. Must make a **credible, measurable case** for why the behaviour would actually change

### Explicitly NOT required
**No real sensors, hardware, or live data.** You may invent or mock any inputs — manual check-ins,
photos, self-reporting, or a simple simulated feed. *"This is a human-behaviour challenge, not a
hardware or analytics one."*

## Judging criteria (4)

| Criterion | What a strong submission shows |
|---|---|
| **Behaviour-change potential** | A credible, well-reasoned path from using this to a person genuinely changing what they do. Mechanic deliberate and grounded in how people actually act — **"not just a badge stuck on a dashboard"** |
| **Measurability** | A specific behaviour and a clear way to measure change: **a baseline, a metric, and a target.** Could you tell *with evidence* whether it worked, rather than assuming it did? |
| **Stickiness & retention** | Would the behaviour hold **after the novelty wears off**? Habit loops, social hooks, defaults, progression — sustaining change rather than a one-day spike |
| **Craft & usability** | Well made and genuinely usable end to end. Clear and low-friction for the chosen audience |

Rubric weightings to be confirmed by the committee.

## Deliverables

- **A working prototype** — web app, mobile app, game, or chat/bot interface — that a real person
  could actually pick up and use
- **A 2–3 minute demo video** showing the experience end to end, **ideally with someone actually
  using or playing it**
- **A brief explanation** of target audience, the specific behaviour being shifted, the
  behaviour-change mechanic, and how you would measure whether it works

### Example directions they suggest
- A challenge/commitment app where friends, classes or teams compete to cut waste, water or
  energy, with progress tracked **against a clear baseline**
- A well-timed nudge or default (a reminder, a pre-commitment, a smart prompt **at the point of
  decision**) that makes the sustainable choice the easy or automatic one

## Additional notes (read these twice)

- This is a **behaviour-change and design challenge**, not a hardware, sensor, or data-analytics
  challenge.
- **"A technically heavy backend will not earn extra points if it does not move behaviour."**
- What matters most is a **credible, measurable case that your solution changes what people
  actually do**; engagement and polish serve that goal, they are not the goal.
- Any domain welcome (energy, water, waste, food, transport, consumption). Any platform welcome
  (web, mobile, game engine, bot).
- They are looking for **insight into behaviour, creativity, and evidence of real impact**.
- Mentors on site throughout — **Glenn from Ecovolt wrote this brief. Talk to him.**

## Guiding questions they posed

1. Think of a time your own behaviour changed because of how something was designed — a streak you
   did not want to break, a default you never changed, a nudge that caught you at the right
   moment. What made it work, and how could that mechanic point at a sustainable habit?
2. People rarely change habits alone, but they will for friends, teams, or bragging rights. How
   could you use social proof, commitment, or friendly rivalry — and how would you know it worked?
3. **Novelty wears off fast and most habit apps are abandoned within a week. What sustains the
   behaviour on day 30, not just day 1?** And be honest: what would actually change what people do
   in real life versus what only sounds good in a pitch?
